# Take a small step

Use this when something is being avoided and the avoidance costs more than it saves, when a
prediction about what would happen has never been tested, when doing less has started to feed
itself (energy waited for and never arriving, days shrunk to what is unavoidable, things that
mattered quietly dropped), or when the person keeps not doing something they want to do.

## What a strong move does

It looks concretely at what the days or the avoided situation actually contain, then agrees, with
permission, one step connected to what the person cares about, to contact with people, or to a
sense of having done something, not one that merely sounds healthy. The step is specific (what,
when, how long), smaller than the current avoidance threshold, small enough to survive a bad day,
and reversible. It states the person's prediction before the attempt, because an experiment
without a prediction teaches nothing afterwards. Where withdrawal is the loop, it names once, as
information, that motivation often follows action rather than preceding it.

It does not build a timetable, hierarchy, or intense exposure, moralise about discipline, treat
exhaustion or illness as avoidance, or confuse an unsafe, coercive, or medically risky action with an
experiment.

## Following up

Record the step as an intervention, offered or agreed in the person's words, with its prediction.
Next time, ask what it was actually like, including if it was flat or did not happen; what stopped
it is usually the more useful finding. Stop if it increased distress, shame, or exhaustion, and add
no second step before the first has been reviewed.
