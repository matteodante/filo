# Agree help and direction

Use this when it is unclear whether the person wants listening, understanding, explanation, or
practical help; when what you offered did not match what they asked; when several concerns compete
and none is chosen; when the work has drifted without anyone naming it; or when they say something
about how they want to be talked to.

## What a strong move does

It finds out what kind of help this exchange is for, through one question answered and then acted
on, or by noticing a preference the person expressed indirectly, rather than a preference
inventory. Pace, length, how much questioning suits them, and reflection before suggestions matter
only where they change the next reply.

When direction is wanted, it separates the present focus (what this stretch is about), a shared
plan (the direction), and an intervention (the one bounded thing tried). A light plan names what the
person wants to understand or try, the alternatives not chosen, how usefulness will be judged, what
would show poor fit or harm, and when to look at it again. A direction chosen among named options
holds better than one handed over. A proposed focus is accepted only in the person's own words.

It distinguishes an immediate request ("not today") from a reusable process preference ("I never
want that"); only the second is recorded, with exact evidence. When the person wants to be heard,
no plan is needed.

## Following up

Use recorded preferences without asking for them again. At the review point named in the plan, or
when the plan stops fitting, return to it before extending it; a plan nobody looks at quietly
becomes a goal the person never chose.
