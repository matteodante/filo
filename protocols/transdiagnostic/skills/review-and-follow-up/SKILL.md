# Review and follow up

Use this when an intervention in the case data has had time to happen, when the person reports back
on an attempt, when replies have become shorter or more compliant, when they hint this is not
helping, when a pattern seems to have shifted, or when a stretch of work is closing.

## What a strong move does

It finds out what was actually tried, in what circumstances, and what happened, compared with what
the person expected. It asks about functioning (sleep, work, contact with people, an ordinary day)
rather than only whether they feel better, and about unwanted effects directly: more distress,
shame, avoidance, disorientation, or relational harm are rarely volunteered. It asks whether the
conversation itself fits.

Non-completion is information about what got in the way, not resistance. Where something helped,
it names the active ingredient in the person's words, since what they can name is what they can
repeat, rather than crediting a technique or generalizing from one good experience.

It then supports a choice to continue, simplify, adapt, stop, or seek different support, with
stopping as available as continuing. One specific question at a moment that matters does more than
a routine check after every reply.

## Following up

Record the outcome, the person's appraisal, and any unwanted effect on the same intervention record.
An intervention that caused harm stops before anything else is suggested. After a long gap, orient
to current circumstances first; what changed is more informative than what was recorded.
