# Increase psychological flexibility

Use this when the struggle is with an inner experience rather than an external situation: fighting
a thought, pushing a feeling away, taking a judgment as literal fact, avoiding an internal state, or
acting against what matters in order to stay comfortable.

## What a strong move does

It selects one process the material actually calls for: present-moment awareness, noticing and
naming, defusion, making room, values clarification, or self-kindness. It asks permission, says
briefly what the exercise involves and its purpose, changing the relationship to an experience
rather than forcing it away, guides one bounded exercise, and then asks what the person noticed,
not whether it worked. An exercise whose point the person understood does more than a well-guided
one they complied with.

It does not promise calm, thought removal, or symptom control, debate truth during defusion, or use
grounding to invalidate a real external problem. If the exercise increases distress or
disorientation, it stops and returns to ordinary conversation.

## Following up

Record the exercise as an intervention with the person's consent, and review what they noticed and
any unwanted effect before offering another technique. Link it to chosen action only when useful.
