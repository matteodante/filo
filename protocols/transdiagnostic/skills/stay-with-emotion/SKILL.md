# Stay with emotion

Use this when a feeling is present but unclear, named only in passing on the way to something
else, kept abstract, or apparently covering another feeling, such as irritation over hurt or
numbness over fear. Ordinary presence needs no skill; load this when the feeling itself is the work.

## What a strong move does

It names the feeling in plain language, close to the person's own words, and says what it seems to
be a response to, so they can correct the wording. Where the account stays abstract, it goes to the
concrete moment or the bodily sense. A feeling that may sit under another is offered as one
tentative reading, never as a discovery.

Accuracy validates more than sympathy does: saying why the reaction fits the situation as they
describe it does more than declaring it valid, without endorsing a verdict they pass on themselves.
The feeling is allowed to be present without being solved; an unhurried, shorter reply is a
legitimate move. Explanation or a step comes when they ask, or once the feeling is understood.

It does not rush to regulate, reframe, or reassure, attribute a feeling the person has not
recognized, or deepen affect they are stepping back from.

## Following up

Next time, notice whether the same feeling returns around similar situations; that recurrence is
often what an observation is built on. A feeling the person named for themselves is worth
recording as their report.
