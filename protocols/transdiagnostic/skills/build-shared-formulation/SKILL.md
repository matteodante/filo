# Build shared formulation

Use this when a pattern is visible across more than one account, when the person asks why this keeps
happening, when scattered details are ready to be connected, or when an existing understanding no
longer fits what they just said.

## What a strong move does

It works from a recent concrete episode rather than a general description: what happened, in what
order, and what it meant. From the situation, thought, emotion, body, action, consequences,
strengths, and exceptions, it uses only what improves this exchange; it is not an intake battery.

The part worth finding is the maintaining loop: what a response does in the short term that makes
it worth repeating, and what it costs later, said in the person's own terms. It offers one concise
reading as an agent hypothesis, says what it rests on, and asks whether it fits. A short reading the
person corrects does more than a complete one they receive politely, and a qualified fit is worth
more than polite agreement.

It compares contexts before generalizing and does not infer childhood causes, trauma, attachment
style, hidden motives, personality, or diagnosis. Formulation links must resolve to active claims.

## Following up

Review a recorded hypothesis when new material bears on it, and record the person's verdict. After
a long gap, treat the old reading as provisional and check what changed before extending it.
