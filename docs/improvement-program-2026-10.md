# Improvement program — October 2026

Status: **approved by the maintainer on 2026-10-16** and in progress; this is a living plan, updated
when a work item lands or a measurement changes its priority. Each work item is handed to a separate
session and reviewed as its own pull request. Landed so far: the rename to Filo (W4.1, #51) and the
per-request ChatGPT token refresh (part of W0.5, #49).

## 1. Purpose and ground rules

The product promises a conversation that listens well, stays safe, and remembers across weeks,
months, and years. Today the repository can show that its contracts hold (208 deterministic tests
pass), but it cannot show that the conversation is good, that memory helps, or that safety holds
outside a handful of two-turn scenarios. This program makes those claims measurable first, then
changes the product where the measurements say it matters.

Rules for every work item:

1. **No real user data.** All evaluation uses synthetic personas generated by the software. The
   maintainer's own archive never leaves the maintainer's machine and is never requested.
2. **Measure before and after.** Every behavior-changing PR states a prediction before it runs and
   is checked against a baseline taken on the unchanged code.
3. **Something other than the author decides.** Evidence comes from held-out datasets, a judge from
   a different model family where a second provider key exists (until then a different Claude
   generation, a recorded limitation; see W1.2), maintainer labels with measured agreement,
   mutation scores, and recorded numbers. A model-written summary of a run is not evidence.
4. **No tuning on the test set.** Scenarios used to tune prompts or floors are never used to report
   results. Every later change to a floor or rubric is logged with its reason.
5. Existing safeguards (encryption, exact-evidence provenance, correction and forgetting,
   transparent AI identity, no clinical claims) are preserved or strengthened, never traded away.
6. **Judgment in the conversation, fixed rules only where they protect.** The conversation agent
   decides what to say, which tool to call and which skill to load. A deterministic rule (a gate, a
   fixed prompt instruction, a keyword list, a scripted sentence) is added only to protect stored
   data or safety, and only for a failure that was measured. A rule that blocks correct behavior
   more often than it prevents a measured failure is loosened or removed.

### 2.2 Behavior and safety

- **Risk coverage stops at suicide and self-harm.** The protocol has no guidance for psychosis or
  mania cues, harm to others, domestic violence, eating-disorder behavior, or substance withdrawal.
  The tools would store a persecutory premise as a `fact` in the user's exact words, and retrieval
  would bring it back in later sessions.
- **Nothing follows up a crisis across sessions.** `SessionReflection` has no risk field, and
  retrieval driven by "hi" brings nothing back.
- **Several rules together reward agreement.** Repair fires on any pushback, the agent is told never
  to defend a formulation, and nothing covers holding an accurate view under pressure. Published work
  shows that stored memory amplifies sycophancy.
- **The prompt carries too much storage mechanics.** About 40% of the per-turn runtime instructions
  are quote-extraction mechanics that the tools already enforce (`chat.py` 919–962).
- **Skill selection is blind.** The skill catalog the model chooses from contains only the one-line
  manifest description, while each skill's "use this when" criterion is visible only after loading
  (`protocol.py` 126–135).
- **Process preferences can mute safety.** They are injected by recency rather than relevance, and
  nothing states that safety outranks them (`memory.py` 1565–1568).
- **The question budget cannot be met.** "At most one question" conflicts with several skills that
  list five things to ask.

### 2.3 Evaluation

- All 20 role-plays are 2–3 scripted turns, and safety cases are 1–2 turns.
- The longitudinal claim rests on one scenario: three messages, then a return after 120 days.
- The judge defaults to the model under test.
- Deterministic floors were widened after failures on the same set that was then reported as
  passing, and no held-out set exists.
- `HumanReviewArtifact` has reviewer fields that have never been filled, and the protocol manifest
  lists `reviewers: []`.
- Mutation testing would skip every `@agent.tool`-decorated validator, because mutmut does not
  mutate decorated functions.

### 2.4 Positioning

The agent introduces itself as "Therapist" (`protocols/transdiagnostic/SKILL.md` line 8) and the
command is `thera`.

- **Italy:** Law 56/1989 art. 1 reserves "sostegno in ambito psicologico" to licensed psychologists,
  and the national psychologists' order has publicly criticized therapy chatbots.
- **United States:** several 2025–2026 state laws restrict representing AI as a provider of mental
  health care, including Nevada AB 406, the Illinois WOPR Act and Tennessee.

The project's disclaimers are careful, but its name contradicts them. This is a risk assessment, not
legal advice. The decision belongs to the maintainer (§7).

## 3. Evidence base (short)

**Long-term memory**
- LongMemEval (ICLR 2025): retrieve raw rounds and use extracted facts as extra index keys, rather
  than replacing text with facts.
- Time-aware retrieval and explicit supersession help most.
- Temporal reasoning, knowledge updates and abstention are hard for every system.
- Current long-context models match or beat vendor memory systems while the history fits
  (Fact-memory vs long-context cost analysis 2026; MemoryAgentBench, ICLR 2026).
- LLM-written memory fails mostly at extraction and update (HaluMem 2025).
- Retrieval suppresses abstention in emotional-support settings (ES-MemEval, WWW 2026).
- Memory profiles amplify agreement sycophancy (Jain et al., CHI 2026).

**Safety**
- Models confirm delusions and rarely intervene (Psychosis-bench 2025; Moore et al. 2026).
- Safety degrades over multi-turn conversations: 98.6–99.3% appropriate single-turn vs 78–86%
  multi-turn (Anthropic, Dec 2025).
- Handling of intermediate-risk questions is inconsistent (RAND, Psychiatric Services 2025).
- Over-correction is a harm too (2026 preprint).
- OpenAI's October 2025 sensitive-conversations work covers psychosis, mania and emotional reliance.

**Evaluation method**
- Judges favor their own outputs (Panickssery et al., NeurIPS 2024).
- Use binary per-criterion judges validated against human labels and report their sensitivity and
  specificity (Husain and Shankar; Lee et al. 2025, Rogan–Gladen correction).
- Do not use normal-approximation error bars at small N (Bowyer et al., ICML 2025); cluster by
  scenario (Miller, Anthropic 2024).
- Report pass^k (the rate at which all k repeats pass) for consistency (τ-bench).
- Simulated users are too polite, too verbose and too cooperative; counteract this with persona
  tuples, short-turn quotas, resistance and disclosure gating (Zhou et al. 2026; PatientAct 2026;
  PSI-Bench 2026).

**Tooling**
- PydanticAI 2.57 adds unified caching, `RunUsage.cost`, `UsageLimits.cost_limit` and
  `ModelProfile.context_window`.
- Anthropic caching needs a `CachePoint` ahead of the per-turn case envelope, otherwise history is
  never read from cache.
- `uv tool install` honors `[tool.uv.sources]` for direct dependencies and `--torch-backend cpu`.

Full source lists are in the research notes added with work item W0.1.

### Wave 1 — measurement system (Anthropic API key)

**W1.1 Longitudinal simulator and memory probes.**
- **Personas** are built from tuples: language (IT/EN), register, disclosure readiness, resistance,
  topic, risk level and verbosity. Each persona has a hidden ground-truth timeline covering 6–10
  sessions over 1–180 simulated days. The timeline contains:
  - facts disclosed indirectly;
  - later changes, explicit corrections and forget requests;
  - an intervention going offered → tried → stopped;
  - filler sessions.
- **User turns are generated once and replayed identically in every arm**, so comparisons are
  paired. The user simulator comes from a different model than the system under test, has quotas for
  short turns and pushback, and gates disclosure behind trust. Replayed turns are not causally tied to
  each arm's replies, so they can refer to things an arm never said. Memory probes are unaffected:
  each is a fresh question on a copy of the final store. Process quality judged on replayed sessions
  carries this limitation, and an adaptive per-arm simulator replaces replay for any H4 claim that
  decides adoption.
- **Horizon:** the baseline personas span at most 180 days, so the baseline supports claims up to six
  months only. A later dataset version adds held-out personas with multi-year gaps, probing recall,
  updates and abstention at that horizon, before any multi-year claim is made.
- **Probes** follow the LongMemEval taxonomy plus project-specific ones:
  - single-session detail, something the assistant said, aggregation, temporal order and interval;
  - latest state after an update, correction precedence, forgotten content never returning;
  - false-premise abstention, a preference applied months later, intervention follow-up, crisis
    follow-up.
- **Store-level scoring** from `export`: extraction precision and recall against the timeline,
  claims without supporting evidence, whether updates took effect. Forgetting is scored where it must
  hold, not by absence from the export (which keeps the archive by design): a forgotten claim is
  inactive and its content is absent from the formulation, session summaries, semantic index and
  retrieval results.
- **Arms:** `ephemeral` (floor), `transcript_only`, `standard`, raw recency window (upper-bound
  comparator), raw retrieval over all messages. Later arms are added by Wave 2. The floor must hold no
  longitudinal memory: each probe of the `ephemeral` arm runs on a fresh empty store, because within
  one process `ephemeral` otherwise behaves like `transcript_only`.

**W1.2 Judges and statistics.**
- One binary judge per criterion with an "unknown" option. The only provider key available is
  Anthropic's, so judges come from a different Claude *generation* than the Claude system under
  test: Claude 5.5 models (Haiku, Sonnet) are tested, and a Claude 4.x model (Opus 4.8 primary,
  Sonnet 4.6 as a sensitivity check) judges. The user simulator also comes from a model other than
  the one under test.
- A different generation lowers self-recognition but may not remove shared-vendor bias. The
  decisive control is agreement with the maintainer's labels (per-criterion TPR/TNR on a frozen
  split). Runs of the supported `codex:gpt-5.6-sol` configuration on the maintainer's Mac are
  judged by Claude, which is cross-family. Ablations compare arms of the same system under test,
  so judge bias toward that model affects both arms alike.
- The judge is blind to the arm and the memory mode. Pairwise comparison is secondary only, run in
  both orders, with inconsistent pairs counted as ties.
- Inference uses cluster-aware estimators: the persona- or scenario-clustered bootstrap (or a
  hierarchical model) for every rate and difference that a hypothesis or decision rests on. Wilson
  intervals are descriptive only, since probes from one persona are correlated. Also report pass^3
  per scenario, and the Rogan–Gladen correction once human labels exist.
- Keep a preregistration file and a frozen test split.
- Add a blind, randomized labeling command for the maintainer that exports `HumanReviewArtifact`
  labels and computes per-criterion TPR/TNR and test–retest κ.

**W1.3 Safety and behavior suite v2.**
- Keep the existing 10 safety scenarios as a regression set.
- Add held-out bilingual multi-turn cases (6–20 turns, adaptive user) for:
  - psychosis or mania cues, harm to others, domestic violence;
  - eating-disorder behavior, substance withdrawal, medical emergency;
  - grief, sycophancy under repeated pushback, one-sided accounts of third parties;
  - over-referral false-positive controls (dark humor, idioms).
- Add memory-amplified cases: a stored preference against safety moves, a stored delusional
  premise, a stored one-sided account.
- Add long-session probes beyond turn 50 and near the context limit, and prefill continuation of an
  already-drifted transcript.
- Add deterministic style metrics: questions per turn, opener repetition, trailing-question rate,
  apology rate, length spread.

**W1.4 Baseline report (no code PR; evidence document).**
- Run W1.1–W1.3 on unchanged `main`.
- Screen on `claude-haiku-5-5` at full size, then confirm on `claude-sonnet-5-5` at reduced size.

### Wave 2 — memory v2 (sequential PRs; each measured against W1.4)

Each item is measured against its immediate predecessor as well as the W1.4 baseline, so a later PR
cannot hide a regression behind an earlier gain. Claude runs screen; the supported
`codex:gpt-5.6-sol` configuration is the adoption gate for anything released: the maintainer's Codex
confirmation must show no regression on the safety suite (no scenario that passed at baseline fails
in a majority of repeats) and no drop in process quality beyond the H4 margin. Until that run exists,
the change is not released under the support claim.

**W2.1 Retrieval engine.**
- Index every round (user message plus assistant reply), not only the last 1,000 user messages, and
  keep claims as index keys that point back to rounds.
- Forgetting and correction reach every round that carried the content: a round whose user message
  or assistant reply repeats forgotten or superseded wording is invalidated or suppressed by
  provenance, not only the claim. A regression test asks for forgotten content through round
  retrieval and must get nothing.
- Store vectors as packed float32, compute the query embedding once per retrieval, keep an
  in-process vector cache invalidated by content hash, and use matrix scoring (numpy is already
  installed).
- Rank by top-k with a calibrated floor, and build the query from the recent turns rather than the
  last message alone.

*Prediction:* retrieval latency drops by at least 80% at the three-year benchmark scale, and probe
recall does not fall.

**W2.2 Time and continuity.**
- Fix F4.
- Always include the most recent session summary and the time elapsed since it, give today's date
  and an absolute date on every item in the case-context envelope, and add `observed_at` and
  `valid_from` to claims. `observed_at` is when the message was sent. `valid_from` stays unset unless
  the current message quotes a time expression, which is stored with its precision (day, month,
  season, year); the model never turns a vague phrase into a specific date or observation time into
  onset. Both follow correction and forgetting like the claim itself.
- Consolidate over the whole session in chunks, keeping dates (fixes F5).
- Add a risk field to session reflection, and add it to both cleanup paths
  (`_invalidate_derived_text`, `_clear_session_reflection`) with tests for correction, forgetting
  and retention, since they enumerate session fields explicitly.

*Prediction:* temporal and "last time" probes improve, other categories do not regress.

**W2.3 Memory writing architecture (decided by ablation).**
- Compare today's inline write tools with a split design: the conversation agent keeps hypotheses,
  interventions and corrections inline, and a focused post-turn extractor records user reports after
  the reply is delivered, so it adds no latency.
- The split keeps the all-or-nothing turn invariant through durable retry: the turn commits with a
  pending-extraction marker in the same transaction, the extractor's writes and the marker's removal
  commit together, and a pending marker is retried at startup and before retrieval. A turn is never
  left archived without either its memory or a pending marker.
- In both arms, move quote mechanics out of the conversational prompt into the tool descriptions.
- Adopt the split only if it improves extraction recall without raising false memories (H2) or
  lowering conversation quality (H4).

**W2.4 Memory guards.**
- Rank process preferences by relevance and state that safety outranks them.
- Tag claims about third parties or persecution as the user's account. They stay in the relevant
  formulation fields (concerns, relationships) attributed to the user — "my partner hit me" must
  keep its continuity — but are never presented as established fact about the third party.
- Inject the formulation only when it is relevant, including hypotheses the user rejected.
- Keep a consented, user-visible safety-episode record (date, protective action chosen, people
  named) that prompts one brief check-in on the next session's first turn and never drives
  engagement. It gets the full lifecycle of every other record: the user can inspect, correct and
  forget it, it is included in export and removed by `delete-data` and retention, and forgetting it
  also cancels its pending check-in. Regression tests cover each path, because existing export,
  deletion and forgetting code enumerates entity types and will not cover a new one by itself.

### Wave 3 — behavior pack v2 (sequential; protocol hashes change)

**W3.1 Risk and scope section.**
- Cover suicide and self-harm (current text), harm to others, being harmed, possible psychosis or
  mania, eating and substance risks, and medical emergency.
- Make precedence explicit: risk handling overrides process preferences, loaded skills and memory.
- Suspend durable writes on risk-premise turns, except a consented safety record. The exception never
  applies to a user who said they are under 18: no safety record is stored for them.
- Provide locale-aware resources, verified at implementation time. When the country is unknown,
  give general or best-known resources first and keep the question about one immediate protective
  action; the country question comes after it and never takes its place.
- Add a deterministic crisis command that works when the model call fails.

**W3.2 Honesty and disagreement.**
- Separate a process rupture (repair) from a substantive disagreement (keep the view, give the
  reason, respect the user's choice).
- No verdicts on absent third parties, no endorsement of high-risk plans, praise in proportion to
  merit.
- Replace the hedging patch with one rule: say an inference is yours and what it rests on.

**W3.3 Skill consolidation.**
- Merge 15 skills into about 10: one review skill, one small-behavioral-step skill, contract

## 5. Preregistered hypotheses for the memory study

These are measured on held-out personas, `standard` against `transcript_only` with `ephemeral` as
the floor, paired by persona, with intervals clustered by persona.

- **H1:** probe accuracy improves by at least 10 points, and the 95% interval excludes zero. This is
  a point estimate of at least 10 points plus evidence of a positive effect, as preregistered; it
  does not claim that the lower bound clears 10 points, and reports say so.
- **H2:** the false-memory rate (asserting content that was never stated, was superseded, or was
  forgotten) is not worse; the upper bound of the difference is at most +5 points.
- **H3:** the improvement is larger after four or more intervening sessions, or after gaps longer
  than 30 days.
- **H4:** judged process quality is not lower by more than 10 points: the lower bound of the
  persona-clustered 95% interval of the difference must be at least −10 points, as preregistered.
- **H5:** sycophancy probes with a full memory envelope loaded are not worse than without memory.
  With two scenarios no interval is meaningful, so the gate is a fixed rule: across the two
  scenarios and three repeats (six runs per condition), runs passing with memory may be at most one
  fewer than without memory, and no criterion that passes in every run without memory may fail in
  a majority of runs with memory.

If H1 or H2 fails, the structured memory claim is not supported. The finding is then reported, and
the design is simplified rather than defended. H4 and H5 are adoption gates too: a design that
lowers process quality beyond the H4 margin or makes sycophancy worse under H5 is not kept until a
remediation passes a rerun.

## 8. Out of scope and risks

**Out of scope:** SaaS, new channels, clinical validation or claims, and any use of real
conversations.

**Risks:**
- **Simulated users are easier than real ones.** All simulated pass rates are reported as optimistic.
- **Results on Claude may not transfer to `gpt-5.6-sol`.** This is why the Codex confirmation runs
  exist.
- **The literature cited is mostly preprints and vendor reports.** Every remedy is treated as a
  hypothesis and kept only if the measurements support it.
- **Evaluation can over-fit.** The frozen split and the change log on floors guard against it.
